**Issue:** The opening scene showed espresso pouring into a cup from a
camera 7 degrees above the counter, chosen for the long, low window
light. From there the near rim hid the inside of the cup below about 4 cm,
so for most of the pour the stream ran into what looked like an empty
cup. The coffee appeared only in the last moments. When the owner asked for the pour to look "really good and realistic",
the reference video showed what was missing: its camera looks down at
about 25-30 degrees, so the crema and the swirl where the stream lands
are in view the whole time. Raising the camera to 19 degrees kept the
window light and showed the cup filling.

The scene had been tuned for many rounds (light, focus, materials)
without anyone checking the one thing the shot is about: whether the
action is visible from the camera.

**Suggested improvement:**

- Candidate skill: before tuning a shot of an action, write down what the
  viewer must see (here: the coffee rising, the crema forming) and check
  from the camera that it is visible for the length of the action, by a
  quick sightline sum or a contact sheet of the whole sequence. Compare the
  camera angle with the reference's, not just the look.
- Site blueprint (opening scenes): a contact sheet of the whole opening,
  one frame every quarter second, is the check for "does the story read",
  before any detail work.

**Principle:** Framing decides what the viewer can see before lighting or
materials decide how it looks. Check that the subject's action is in view
for its whole length before polishing anything.
