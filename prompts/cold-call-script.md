# Cold-call script: "I already built you a website"

For calling a local business whose site we've already redesigned (the sites
in `sites/`). Written 2026-10-29 from the owner's draft. The owner also sent
a YouTube video on tonality (youtu.be/KQ6c9vceu7A), but YouTube is blocked
from this container, so the tone notes below are standard cold-call
practice, not taken from the video. Rework this once the video's points are
pasted in.

Brackets are how to say the line, not words to read out.

## Before you dial

- Have the new site's link ready to text (e.g. the live GitHub Pages URL),
  and open their current site so you can name one specific thing that's
  wrong with it.
- Know the owner's name if it's public (Facebook, Google, their About page).
- Call mid-morning or mid-afternoon, not at their rush (cafés: avoid 7-10 AM).
- Stand up and smile while you talk. It comes through in your voice.

## Tone, start to finish

- **Calm and low, not salesy.** Speak a little slower than feels natural.
  Fast and high-pitched sounds like a robocall.
- **Statements go down at the end.** "I built you a website." (down) Not
  "I built you a website?" (up). Rising sounds unsure.
- **Real questions go up, softly.** "Is that cool?" (up, relaxed).
- **Pause after anything important** and let them talk first. Silence is fine.
- **Sound like you're doing them a favour you already finished**, not asking
  for one. The work is done; you're just showing them.
- **Never argue.** If they push back, agree with the feeling, then answer.

## The call

**1. Confirm who you're talking to**
> "Hey, is this [Business name]?" *(friendly, up)*
>
> "Awesome. Is this [Owner's name]?" / "Are you the owner?"

If it's not the owner: "No worries, when's a good time to catch them?" Get
the time, thank them, hang up. Don't pitch the staff.

**2. Who you are, in one breath**
> "Hey [Name], this is [Your name], I'm a web designer here in [city]."
> *(warm, steady, down at the end)*

Local matters. Say the city.

**3. Cut to the chase**
> "I'll be quick, I know you're busy." *(pause)*

**4. The reason you called**
> "I was looking at [Business] on Google Maps, love what you guys do, by the
> way, and when I clicked through to your website, it looked pretty
> outdated." *(matter-of-fact, not insulting, down)*

Then name one specific thing: "the photos are stock", "it's hard to read on
a phone", "the booking page doesn't work", "there's no menu on it". Specific
beats general; it proves you actually looked.

**5. Pause. Let them react.** Don't fill the silence. Whatever they say
("yeah, I know", "I made it myself", "it's fine"), agree with it:
> "Totally, and honestly most places around here are the same."

**6. The hook**
> "So I went ahead and already designed you a new one." *(confident, a beat
> of pause before "already")*
>
> "It's done, it's live, it's got all your info on it: your hours, [your
> menu / your services], your number. Can I text you the link so you can
> see it?" *(easy, up)*

**7. Send it while you're on the phone**
> "Sending it now... you should have it. Take a look, I'll hang on."

Let them scroll. If they say something nice, ask what they like about it. If
they go quiet, ask: "What's your first impression?"

**8. Their opinion first, price second**
> "What do you think? Honest opinion, I can take it." *(relaxed)*

If they like it, go to the price. If they want changes, write them down and
say "easy, that's included."

**9. The price**
> "So here's the deal. If you want it, it's yours for five hundred, one
> time." *(flat, down, then stop talking)*
>
> "For comparison, an agency would usually charge [a few thousand] for
> something like this, and you'd wait weeks. This one's already built."
>
> "Does that work for your budget?" *(up, then silence)*

Whoever talks first after the price usually loses. Wait.

**10. Close or follow up**
- **Yes:** "Awesome. What's the best way to send you the details, text or
  email?" Then agree the next step (payment, domain, changes) and a date.
- **Needs to think / ask a partner:** "Totally fair. I'll leave the link with
  you. Is it cool if I check in [Thursday]?" Get a day.
- **No:** "No problem at all, thanks for taking a look. The link's yours if
  you change your mind." Stay friendly; the site can still be sold later.

End every call with how they want to be reached:
> "What's the best way to get back to you, text, call, or email?"

## Objections

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "Who are you / how did you get my number?" | "It's on your Google listing. I'm local, I build sites for businesses around [city]." |
| "We already have a website." | "Totally, that's actually why I called. Just take a look at the new one side by side, no pressure." |
| "I don't have time." | "Completely get it. Can I text you the link and call back [day] at [time]?" |
| "$500 is too much." | "What would make it work for you?" (Know your floor before you call. Offer a payment split before you drop the price.) |
| "Is this a scam?" | "Fair question. You don't pay anything until you've seen it and you're happy. Here's the link, it's already live." |
| "My nephew does our website." | "Nice. Happy for him to look at it too, the code's clean and easy to work on." |
| "Send me an email." | "For sure, what's the best email?" Then follow up within the hour. |

## Before you say any of this, be sure it's true

- **"It's got all your info"**: check the facts on the new site against their
  current site and Google the day you call (hours change).
- **"Market price"**: only quote a comparison you can back up.
- **What $500 includes**: decide before calling. Hosting (and who pays for
  it), connecting their domain, and how many rounds of changes.
- **The sites are `noindex` and unapproved** until the business says yes;
  that's the right state to show them, but tell them it's a preview link,
  not their new address yet.
- **Texting**: send the link only after they say yes on the call. Texting
  marketing to a cell phone without permission can break US texting rules
  (TCPA), so don't cold-text the pitch.
